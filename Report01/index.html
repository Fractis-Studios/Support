Fractis Studios — Security Audit Report

Generated during investigation of a suspicious invoice email (INV160488).

Trigger

Suspicious invoice email (INV160488) received by Fractis Studios, purportedly from "Adam Ryder Brooker" sent from an obscure/tempmail address (ven200@libero.it), forwarding a fake invoice supposedly from finance@remit-payment.com (Cape Town, South Africa). Daleen forwarded the message to the investigator for review. The info@fractisstudios.com address appears only as the forwarding mailbox, not the original sender.

Email Forensics

  • Authentication: DKIM=pass, DMARC=pass, SPF=pass for fractisstudios.com.
  • Sending infrastructure: Authenticated sender info@fractisstudios.com via Microsoft 365, relayed through Hostinger SMTP.
  • Content: Classic "overdue invoice" phishing. No prior history with remit-payment.com or ven200@libero.it.

Website & DNS

  • Site resolves and serves normally. HTTPS active.
  • DNS A: 88.222.223.48, 5.252.75.6. MX: mx1/mx2.hostinger.com. NS: ns1/ns2.dns-parking.com.
  • SPF: v=spf1 include:_spf.mail.hostinger.com ~all. DKIM present. DMARC: v=DMARC1; p=none (weak, upgrade recommended).

Subdomain & Exposed-Path Scan

  • Subdomains resolving: www, ftp. No dangling CNAMEs.
  • Platform is WordPress. /wp-admin/ and /wp-login.php reachable. /admin/ returns 200.
  • Sensitive files (.env, .git/config, phpinfo, backups, SQL dumps) return 403/404.
  • security.txt not present.

Recommended Actions

  1. Reset passwords; revoke sessions/app passwords.
  2. Enable 2FA on mailbox and hosting panel.
  3. Audit mailbox rules & forwarding.
  4. Check Sent Items.
  5. Review Hostinger DNS & account activity logs.
  6. Scan endpoints for infostealer malware.
  7. Notify contacts the invoice was not sent by Fractis Studios.
  8. Report finance@remit-payment.com as phishing.
  9. Keep WP updated; disable XML-RPC; enforce 2FA; install WAF.
  10. Upgrade DMARC to quarantine/reject.

Socials

Audit each linked social account for unauthorized posts, changed bios/links, new admins, suspicious DMs, connected OAuth apps. Revoke unknown apps, rotate passwords, enable 2FA.

Conclusion

Phishing originated externally via tempmail/obscure domain. No evidence of website defacement or data leak. Prioritize mailbox + hosting account hardening and DMARC upgrade.
